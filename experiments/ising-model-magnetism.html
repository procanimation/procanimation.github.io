Why does a block of ordinary iron suddenly become a permanent magnet when cooled below a precise threshold, yet completely lose all magnetic order when heated? In 1920, Wilhelm Lenz proposed that ferromagnetism could be modeled as a lattice of interacting binary spins ($s_i = \pm 1$). Solved exactly in two dimensions by Lars Onsager in 1944, the Ising Model proved from first principles that microscopic pairwise interactions can spontaneously break symmetry, giving birth to the modern theory of phase transitions, the Renormalization Group, and Hopfield neural networks.

1. The Microscopic Spin Lattice and Detailed Balance

The two-dimensional Ising model is defined on a square lattice with periodic boundary conditions. Each site has a discrete spin $s_i \in \{-1, +1\}$. In the absence of an external magnetic field ($h = 0$), the Hamiltonian is:

The 2D Ising Hamiltonian
$$H(\mathbf{s}) = -J \sum_{\langle i, j \rangle} s_i s_j$$

The probability of finding the system in configuration $\mathbf{s}$ is given by the Boltzmann distribution $P(\mathbf{s}) = \frac{1}{Z} e^{-\beta H(\mathbf{s})}$, where $\beta = \frac{1}{k_B T}$ is the inverse temperature and $Z = \sum_{\mathbf{s}} e^{-\beta H(\mathbf{s})}$ is the partition function.

The Metropolis-Hastings algorithm satisfies detailed balance:

$$P(\mathbf{s}) W(\mathbf{s} \to \mathbf{s}') = P(\mathbf{s}') W(\mathbf{s}' \to \mathbf{s}) \implies \frac{W(\mathbf{s} \to \mathbf{s}')}{W(\mathbf{s}' \to \mathbf{s})} = e^{-\beta \Delta E}$$

2. Lars Onsager's 1944 Analytical Solution

In 1944, Lars Onsager achieved one of the greatest analytical feats in mathematical physics by solving the 2D Ising model exactly using transfer matrices and Lie algebra representations. He derived the exact critical temperature:

$$\sinh\left(\frac{2J}{k_B T_c}\right) = 1 \implies \frac{k_B T_c}{J} = \frac{2}{\ln(1 + \sqrt{2})} \approx 2.269185\dots$$

Below $T_c$, the spontaneous magnetization is non-zero and governed by the critical exponent $\beta = 1/8$:

$$M(T) = \left[ 1 - \sinh^{-4}\left(\frac{2J}{k_B T}\right) \right]^{1/8}$$
