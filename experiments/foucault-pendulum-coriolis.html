In March 1851, beneath the vaulted dome of the Panthéon in Paris, French physicist Jean Bernard Léon Foucault suspended a 28-kilogram brass sphere from a 67-meter steel wire. At its base, a stylus skimmed a bed of wet sand spread across the marble floor. As the crowd watched in silence, each pass of the pendulum scored a new line slightly to the right of the last, tracing a steady clockwise precession. For the first time in history, humanity could directly witness the rotation of the Earth beneath its feet without celestial reference. The phenomenon is governed by non-inertial Coriolis forces, the Sine of Latitude Law, and differential geometry: the pendulum executes parallel transport on the curved surface of a sphere, demonstrating Geometric Holonomy.

The Non-Inertial & Geometric Lexicon

The Coriolis Force (1835)

In a reference frame rotating with Earth's angular velocity $\boldsymbol{\Omega}$, any moving body experiences an apparent sideways acceleration: $\mathbf{a}_{\text{coriolis}} = -2(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \mathbf{v})$. In the Northern Hemisphere, this force continuously deflects moving objects to their right.

The Sine of Latitude Law

Only the component of Earth's rotation perpendicular to the local ground, $\Omega_z = \Omega_{\text{earth}} \sin(\lambda)$, drives the horizontal precession. The period of a complete $360^\circ$ rotation is $T = \frac{24\text{ hours}}{\sin\lambda}$. At the North Pole $T = 24\text{h}$; in Paris $T = 31.8\text{h}$; at the Equator $T = \infty$.

Precessing Rosette Ground Tracks

Because the Coriolis force slightly curves each individual swing into an extremely elongated narrow ellipse, the tip of the pendulum bob sweeps out an intricate, non-repeating multi-petaled rosette pattern across the floor over successive hours.

Holonomy & Gauss-Bonnet on $S^2$

The pendulum's oscillation plane undergoes parallel transport along a circle of constant latitude on the two-sphere $S^2$. The angle deficit after one full terrestrial day equals the solid angle subtended by the latitude circle: $\Delta \alpha = 2\pi(1 - \sin\lambda)$.

The Non-Inertial Reference Frame Derivation

Consider a coordinate frame $(x, y, z)$ affixed to the Earth at latitude $\lambda$, where $\mathbf{e}_x$ points East, $\mathbf{e}_y$ points North, and $\mathbf{e}_z$ points vertically upward along the local zenith. The angular velocity vector of the Earth in this local frame is:

$$\boldsymbol{\Omega} = (0, \, \Omega \cos\lambda, \, \Omega \sin\lambda)$$

The Coriolis acceleration experienced by a pendulum bob moving with velocity $\mathbf{v} = (\dot{x}, \dot{y}, \dot{z})$ is $\mathbf{a}_c = -2(\boldsymbol{\Omega} \times \mathbf{v})$. For small oscillations in the horizontal plane ($\dot{z} \approx 0$):

$$\mathbf{a}_c = 2\Omega (\dot{y}\sin\lambda \, \mathbf{e}_x - \dot{x}\sin\lambda \, \mathbf{e}_y + \dot{x}\cos\lambda \, \mathbf{e}_z)$$

The horizontal equations of motion for a pendulum of length $L$ become:

$$\ddot{x} + \frac{g}{L}x = 2\Omega \sin\lambda \, \dot{y}, \qquad \ddot{y} + \frac{g}{L}y = -2\Omega \sin\lambda \, \dot{x}$$

Introducing the complex coordinate $\xi = x + i y$, the coupled equations collapse into:

$$\ddot{\xi} + 2i\Omega\sin\lambda \, \dot{\xi} + \frac{g}{L}\xi = 0$$

Transforming to a coordinate frame rotating with angular frequency $\omega = -\Omega \sin\lambda$ completely eliminates the Coriolis cross-terms. The pendulum swings as a simple harmonic oscillator in the rotating frame, confirming that its plane of oscillation precesses clockwise at the exact rate $\omega_{\text{prec}} = \Omega \sin\lambda$.
