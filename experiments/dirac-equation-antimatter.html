1. Linearizing the Relativistic Dispersion Relation

Special Relativity dictates the invariant mass-energy relation $E^2 = p^2 c^2 + m^2 c^4$. Replacing energy and momentum with quantum differential operators $E \to i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t}$ and $\mathbf{p} \to -i\hbar \boldsymbol{\nabla}$ leads to the second-order Klein-Gordon equation:

$$\left( \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2} - \nabla^2 + \frac{m^2 c^2}{\hbar^2} \right)\psi = 0$$

To eliminate negative probability densities, Paul Dirac factored the Hamiltonian into a strictly linear first-order differential form:

The Dirac Equation (Covariant Form)
$$(i\hbar \gamma^\mu \partial_\mu - mc)\psi = 0$$

Squaring the operator requires the coefficients $\gamma^\mu$ to satisfy the anticommutation relations of a Clifford algebra:

$$\{\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu\} = \gamma^\mu \gamma^\nu + \gamma^\nu \gamma^\mu = 2\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathbb{I}_4$$

2. The Dirac Sea and Carl Anderson's 1932 Positron

The solutions to the Dirac equation yield four independent orthogonal states for each momentum $\mathbf{p}$: two positive energy states ($E = +\sqrt{p^2 c^2 + m^2 c^4}$) representing spin-up and spin-down electrons, and two negative energy states ($E = -\sqrt{p^2 c^2 + m^2 c^4}$).

Dirac postulated that the vacuum is an infinite sea of electrons occupying all negative energy states. Applying the Pauli exclusion principle, an empty vacancy ("hole") in this negative sea acts as a physical particle with:

$$E_{\text{hole}} = -(-E) = +E, \quad q_{\text{hole}} = -(-e) = +e$$

In 1932, Carl Anderson confirmed Dirac's hypothesis experimentally by photographing the trail of a cosmic ray positron deflected by a magnetic field in a cloud chamber.
