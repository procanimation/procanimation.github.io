If you shoot bullets through two narrow slits in a steel wall, they form two distinct bands on the target. If you send water waves, they pass through both slits, ripple outwards, and create an alternating pattern of constructive crests and destructive cancellations. But if you fire microscopic quantum particles—electrons, photons, or even whole molecules—they behave like waves when left alone, and like particles the instant you try to catch them in the act.

The Quantum Physics Lexicon

Wavefunction ($\psi$)

A complex mathematical function $\psi(x, y, t)$ governed by the Schrödinger equation $i\hbar \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} = \hat{H}\psi$, containing all information about a quantum system.

The Born Rule ($P = |\psi|^2$)

Formulated by Max Born in 1926. The square of the wavefunction's absolute value represents the probability density of discovering the particle at a given point in space.

Quantum Superposition

Before measurement, a particle does not choose Slit 1 or Slit 2. It exists simultaneously in a coherent linear combination of states: $|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\text{Slit 1}\rangle + |\text{Slit 2}\rangle)$.

Wavefunction Collapse & Decoherence

Interacting with an external measurement device extracts "which-way" information, causing environmental decoherence that collapses the quantum superposition into classical probability.
