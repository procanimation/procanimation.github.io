How does a collection of silent mathematical equations make a definitive decision? Inside biological neural circuits, billions of microscopic neurons aggregate electrochemical spikes until a threshold is crossed, firing an action potential to downstream neighbors. In artificial intelligence, this biological miracle is distilled into the foundation of all modern deep learning: The Artificial Neuron and The Sigmoid Activation Function.

The Neural Architecture

The Perceptron Accumulator

$$z = \mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{x} + b = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i x_i + b$$. Incoming features $$\mathbf{x}$$ are scaled by synaptic weights $$\mathbf{w}$$ (excitatory or inhibitory). The bias $$b$$ acts as a physical counterweight setting the baseline excitation threshold.

The Sigmoid Squashing Machine

$$\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}, \quad \sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z))$$. Continuously compresses the infinite real domain $$(-\infty, +\infty)$$ into a strict probability interval $$(0, 1)$$. Its derivative peaks at $$z=0$$ ($$0.25$$), ensuring the model learns fastest when it is most uncertain.

The 2D Probability Field

$$P(y = 1 \mid \mathbf{x}) = \sigma(\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{x} + b)$$. A single neuron paints the entire feature space with a smooth probability manifold. Right down the center lies the decision boundary ($$\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{x} + b = 0$$), where prediction odds are exactly $$50\%$$.

Binary Cross-Entropy & The XOR Trap

$$\mathcal{L} = -[y \ln \hat{y} + (1-y)\ln(1-\hat{y})]$$. Prevents vanishing gradients on saturated plateaus. A single neuron can only draw linear boundaries; solving non-linear patterns like XOR requires stacking neurons into deep layers.
