In classical physics, an object without sufficient kinetic energy can never scale a potential hill: roll a marble up a ramp, and if it lacks energy, it halts and reflects every single time. But in the quantum realm, particles are not hard billiard balls; they are complex probability wavepackets $\Psi(x, t)$ governed by the Schrödinger equation. When a quantum wavepacket collides with a barrier taller than its own energy, the wave does not abruptly terminate. An evanescent wave penetrates into the barrier, decaying exponentially. If the barrier is thin enough, a non-zero portion emerges on the other side. This is Quantum Tunneling: matter walking straight through impenetrable walls.

The Quantum Tunneling Lexicon

Transmission Coefficient ($T$)

For a rectangular barrier of width $L$ and potential $V_0 > E$, the probability of tunneling decays exponentially: $T \approx 16\frac{E}{V_0}\left(1 - \frac{E}{V_0}\right) e^{-2\kappa L}$, where $\kappa = \frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}}{\hbar}$ is the attenuation constant.

Gamow's Miracle (Alpha Decay)

In 1928, George Gamow explained nuclear alpha decay. Trapped behind the nuclear Coulomb barrier, an alpha particle collides $10^{21}$ times per second against the wall, each bounce carrying a minuscule tunneling probability. This explains why nuclear half-lives span from microseconds to billions of years.

The Solar Core Paradox

Protons inside the Sun's core at 15 million Kelvin have an average thermal energy of only $\approx 1\,\text{keV}$, yet the Coulomb repulsion barrier is $\approx 1000\,\text{keV}$. Without quantum tunneling, protons would never fuse, the Sun would remain cold and dark, and life on Earth could not exist.

Scanning Tunneling Microscope (STM)

Invented at IBM in 1981 by Binnig and Rohrer (Nobel Prize 1986). A metallic needle ground to a single atom scans across a surface. Electrons tunnel across the vacuum gap. Because $I \propto e^{-2\kappa d}$, a distance change of just $0.1\,\text{nm}$ alters current by an entire order of magnitude.

The Mathematics of Penetration

Inside a classically forbidden barrier where potential energy $V_0$ exceeds total kinetic energy $E$, the time-independent Schrödinger equation takes the form:

$$\frac{d^2 \psi(x)}{dx^2} = \kappa^2 \psi(x), \quad \text{where} \quad \kappa = \frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}}{\hbar}$$

Unlike oscillating sinusoidal solutions $e^{ikx}$ in free space, the solution inside the barrier is a real exponential decay:

$$\psi_{\text{barrier}}(x) = A e^{-\kappa x} + B e^{\kappa x}$$

Because $e^{-\kappa x}$ never reaches absolute zero over a finite thickness $L$, the wavefunction retains a small non-zero amplitude at the far boundary $x = L$. It couples back into a freely propagating sinusoidal wave, carrying real momentum and probability out into the universe.
