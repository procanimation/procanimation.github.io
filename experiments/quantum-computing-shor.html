For seventy years, digital computing has obeyed the binary laws of classical information: a bit is either zero or one. In 1981, Richard Feynman observed that classical machines are fundamentally unsuited to simulate quantum physics because nature itself is quantum mechanical. By harnessing quantum superposition, entanglement, and wave interference, a quantum computer processes an exponential multiverse of states simultaneously. In 1994, Peter Shor delivered the ultimate proof of this power: an algorithm capable of cracking the world's cryptographic bedrock in minutes.

The Quantum Information Lexicon

The Qubit & The Bloch Sphere

A quantum bit exists in a continuous linear superposition $|\psi\rangle = \cos(\theta/2)|0\rangle + e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2)|1\rangle$. Geometrically mapped onto the unit Bloch Sphere, $\theta$ controls relative probability amplitudes while $\phi$ controls quantum phase.

Exponential Entangled Multiverse

Two classical bits represent only one of 4 states at a given instant. Two entangled qubits hold all 4 states at once; $n$ qubits hold $2^n$ coherent amplitudes simultaneously. A register of just 300 qubits holds more simultaneous states than all atoms in the observable universe.

Shor's Algorithm & The QFT

In 1994, Peter Shor mapped prime factorization into period finding of $f(x) = a^x \pmod N$. By passing the entangled register through a Quantum Fourier Transform, destructive interference cancels wrong outputs while constructive interference amplifies the true period $r$, cracking RSA in polynomial time $O((\log N)^3)$.

Decoherence & Surface Codes

Superposition wavefunctions are delicate: stray thermal photons cause decoherence. Quantum Error Correction protects information by entangling thousands of physical noisy qubits into a single topological logical qubit, repairing phase errors without collapsing the wavefunction.

How Shor's Algorithm Breaks RSA in Polynomial Time

The mathematical security of RSA relies on the computational asymmetry between multiplication and prime factorization:

  • Classical Complexity (GNFS): The best known classical algorithm—the General Number Field Sieve—scales sub-exponentially as $\mathcal{O}\left(\exp\left(c \sqrt[3]{\ln N (\ln \ln N)^2}\right)\right)$. Factoring a 2048-bit key requires billions of core-years.
  • Quantum Complexity (Shor's Algorithm): Peter Shor's quantum circuit solves period finding via the Quantum Fourier Transform in polynomial time $\mathcal{O}((\log N)^3)$, reducing billions of years to mere minutes on a fault-tolerant machine.
