In 1935, Albert Einstein, Boris Podolsky, and Nathan Rosen thought they had dealt a lethal blow to quantum mechanics. They envisioned two entangled particles flying across the cosmos. If measuring particle A on Earth immediately dictates the outcome of particle B light-years away, either instantaneous "spooky action at a distance" is real, or the particles must carry secret instructions—local hidden variables—from the moment they formed. For three decades, the debate was dismissed as unprovable philosophy. Then, in 1964, John Stewart Bell discovered a mathematical inequality that could be tested in a laboratory.

The Non-Locality Lexicon

The Singlet State ($|\Psi^-\rangle$)

An entangled pair of spin-1/2 particles whose total angular momentum is zero: $|\Psi^-\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\!\uparrow\downarrow\rangle - |\!\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)$. Neither particle possesses a definite spin until a measurement axis is chosen.

The CHSH Inequality ($|S| \le 2$)

Formulated by Clauser, Horne, Shimony, and Holt in 1969. In any universe ruled by Local Realism (pre-existing properties + speed-of-light signaling), the correlation parameter $S$ can never exceed $2$.

Tsirelson's Bound ($2\sqrt{2}$)

Quantum mechanics violates the classical limit $2$, reaching a maximum quantum correlation of $S = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.8284$. The 2022 Nobel Prize (Aspect, Clauser, Zeilinger) confirmed this violation beyond any reasonable doubt.

The No-Signaling Theorem

Even though wavefunction collapse is instantaneous across cosmic distances, the outcome of any individual measurement is fundamentally random. Without a classical speed-of-light phone call to compare notes, no faster-than-light signal can ever be sent.

Why Bell's Inequality Changed Reality

When Alice and Bob measure their particles along axes $a$ and $b$, quantum mechanics predicts an angular correlation:

$$E(a, b) = -\cos(a - b)$$

Notice how curved the cosine function is. Classical probability models based on hidden variables can only generate linear correlation slopes. Under optimal test angles ($a = 0^\circ, a' = 90^\circ, b = 45^\circ, b' = 135^\circ$), the difference between adjacent angles is always $45^\circ$, where $-\cos(45^\circ) = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \approx -0.7071$. Summing the four terms gives:

$$S = |E(a, b) - E(a, b') + E(a', b) + E(a', b')| = \left| -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right| = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.8284$$

Because $2.8284$ strictly exceeds the classical upper bound of $2$, local realism is mathematically refuted. The universe does not permit local hidden variables.
