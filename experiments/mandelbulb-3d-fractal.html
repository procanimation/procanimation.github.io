The Mandelbrot set is universally celebrated as the crown jewel of mathematical visualization. Yet for three decades following Benoît Mandelbrot's 1980 breakthrough, the fractal remained strictly confined to the two-dimensional complex plane $\mathbb{C}$. In three dimensions, complex multiplication breaks down entirely: Ferdinand Georg Frobenius proved in 1877 that the only associative division algebras over the real numbers are $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, and four-dimensional quaternions $\mathbb{H}$—no three-dimensional number system exists. In 2009, amateur mathematician Daniel White and fractal developer Paul Nylander bypassed this algebraic wall using spherical triplex coordinates, constructing the breathtaking 3D Mandelbulb.

The 3D Fractal & Raymarching Lexicon

The Frobenius Theorem (1877)

In two dimensions, complex numbers $z = x + i y$ multiply naturally via $i^2 = -1$, preserving length and angle. However, Frobenius proved that it is algebraically impossible to define a 3-dimensional division algebra where multiplication is both associative and preserves Euclidean lengths. Three-dimensional fractals cannot be produced through standard algebraic fields.

The White-Nylander Spherical Triplex

White and Nylander bypassed Frobenius by converting Cartesian points $(x, y, z)$ into spherical polar coordinates $(r, \theta, \phi)$: $$r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}, \quad \theta = \arccos(z / r), \quad \phi = \arctan(y / x)$$ Raising the triplex vector to power $n$ rotates the polar and azimuthal angles by $n$: $$v^n = r^n \begin{pmatrix} \sin(n\theta)\cos(n\phi) \\ \sin(n\theta)\sin(n\phi) \\ \cos(n\theta) \end{pmatrix}$$

The Power-8 Iteration: $v_{k+1} = v_k^8 + c$

Starting at initial position $v_0 = (0, 0, 0)$ and adding constant point $c = (x, y, z)$, the sequence is iterated: $$v_{k+1} = v_k^8 + c$$ While power $n=2$ yields an uninteresting apple-shaped bulb, setting $n=8$ produces spectacular, self-similar 8-fold flower symmetry, Romanesque domes, and infinite Gothic spires.

Sphere Tracing & Distance Estimation (SDF)

Because the Mandelbulb is infinitely detailed, rasterizing polygons is impossible. Instead, computer graphics uses Raymarching (Sphere Tracing). Along each camera ray, the distance to the fractal boundary is estimated using the chain rule derivative $dr$: $$d(p) \approx \frac{1}{2} \frac{|v| \ln |v|}{|dr|}$$ This Signed Distance Function (SDF) allows rays to take massive, safe geometric steps through empty space without overshooting.

Hausdorff Fractal Dimension ($D \approx 2.85$)

The surface area of the Mandelbulb is non-differentiable and divergent. The boundary possesses an estimated fractional Hausdorff dimension of $D \approx 2.85$, meaning it occupies nearly the full volume of three-dimensional space while remaining bounded inside a sphere of radius $R = 2.0$.
