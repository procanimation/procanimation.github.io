In 1822, French mathematician and physicist Jean-Baptiste Joseph Fourier introduced an audacious claim to the French Academy of Sciences: any periodic mathematical function or arbitrary curve, no matter how jagged or discontinuous, can be synthesized as a sum of pure sinusoidal frequencies. His contemporaries, including Lagrange and Laplace, initially scoffed at the idea. Today, the Fourier Transform underpins modern digital existence: JPEG compression, MP3 audio, MRI imaging, quantum wave mechanics, and telecommunications.

The Harmonic Analysis Lexicon

Harmonic Epicycles

A chain of rotating vectors linked head-to-tail, each orbiting at integer multiples of a fundamental frequency ($\omega, 2\omega, 3\omega, \dots$).

Orthogonality of Sine & Cosine

Trigonometric basis functions are mutually orthogonal over a period: $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \sin(mx)\sin(nx)\,dx = \pi \delta_{mn}$. This isolates each frequency independently.

The Gibbs Phenomenon

At jump discontinuities, the Fourier series overshoots the true function value by $\approx 8.95\%$, an inescapable artifact of truncating an infinite trigonometric basis.

Time vs. Frequency Duality

The Fourier transform provides a bidirectional mathematical lens: time-domain oscillations on one side, and spectral frequency spikes on the other.

1. The Mathematical Foundation

Any periodic function $f(t)$ with period $T$ and fundamental frequency $\omega_0 = \frac{2\pi}{T}$ can be expressed as a linear combination of infinite sines and cosines:

General Fourier Series
$$f(t) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left[ a_n \cos(n \omega_0 t) + b_n \sin(n \omega_0 t) \right]$$ $$\text{where} \quad a_n = \frac{2}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) \cos(n \omega_0 t)\,dt, \quad b_n = \frac{2}{T} \int_{0}^{T} f(t) \sin(n \omega_0 t)\,dt$$

2. Deconstructing the Square Wave

Because the ideal square wave of period $2\pi$ is an odd function ($f(-t) = -f(t)$), all cosine coefficients $a_n$ vanish identically to zero ($a_n = 0$). Evaluating the sine coefficients:

Square Wave Fourier Synthesis
$$b_n = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(t) \sin(nt)\,dt = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin(nt)\,dt = \frac{2}{n\pi} [1 - (-1)^n]$$ $$\text{For even } n: \quad b_n = 0. \qquad \text{For odd } n: \quad b_n = \frac{4}{n\pi}.$$ $$f_{\text{square}}(t) = \frac{4}{\pi} \left( \sin(\omega t) + \frac{1}{3}\sin(3\omega t) + \frac{1}{5}\sin(5\omega t) + \frac{1}{7}\sin(7\omega t) + \dots \right)$$
