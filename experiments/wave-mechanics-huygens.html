When a stone falls into still water, no drop travels from the center to the bank; instead, energy and momentum ripple outward across the liquid plane while the molecules themselves merely bob in place. From the sweet harmonics of a cello string and the thunderous pressure crack of a supersonic sonic boom to the wave-particle duality of quantum probability amplitudes, wave mechanics is nature's fundamental mechanism for transporting energy across spacetime without net transport of matter.

The Wave Mechanics Architecture

The Classical Wave Equation

$$\frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial t^2} = v^2 \frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial x^2}$$. Wave speed is dictated strictly by the physical medium: $$v = \lambda f = \sqrt{T/\mu}$$. Transverse disturbances displace perpendicular to propagation; longitudinal disturbances displace parallel, generating alternating acoustic compressions and rarefactions.

Superposition & Standing Resonance

$$y_{\text{net}} = 2A \sin(kx) \cos(\omega t)$$. Because the master wave equation is linear, passing waves sum algebraically. When identical waves travel in opposing directions along a bounded medium, boundary reflections lock into standing harmonic modes characterized by stationary nodes ($$y = 0$$) and vibrating antinodes.

The Doppler Shift & Mach Cone

$$f' = f_0 \left(\frac{v_{\text{snd}}}{v_{\text{snd}} \mp v_{\text{src}}}\right)$$. Moving wave sources compress wavefronts ahead into higher frequencies (blue shift) and dilate them behind. Exceeding the wave speed ($$v > c$$) bunches all emitted wavefronts into a conical shock boundary: the Mach cone ($$\sin \theta = 1/M$$), unleashing a sonic boom.

The Huygens-Fresnel Principle

$$n_1 \sin \theta_1 = n_2 \sin \theta_2$$. Every point on an advancing wavefront acts as an independent secondary emitter of spherical wavelets. The forward envelope tangent to all wavelets constitutes the subsequent wavefront, geometrically deducing Snell's law of refraction and wave diffraction around obstacles.
