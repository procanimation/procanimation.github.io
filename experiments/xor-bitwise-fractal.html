In digital electronics, the Exclusive OR (XOR) gate performs the most elemental decision: output true if and only if its inputs disagree. In binary arithmetic, XOR is addition without carry. Yet when you evaluate this tiny binary operator across a two-dimensional grid of integers, a staggering phenomenon emerges: infinite recursive self-similarity, the iconic Sierpinski Gasket, and crystalline tapestries that rival ancient sacred geometry.

The Discrete Mathematics Lexicon

Bitwise Exclusive OR ($\oplus$)

A fundamental binary operation. In the finite field $\mathbb{F}_2$ (Galois field of two elements), $x \oplus y$ computes addition modulo 2: $0 \oplus 0 = 0$, $0 \oplus 1 = 1$, $1 \oplus 0 = 1$, and $1 \oplus 1 = 0$.

Sierpinski Triangle Emergence

Plotting all coordinate pairs $(x, y)$ satisfying $(x \ \& \ y) = 0$ or $(x \oplus y) < \text{threshold}$ produces the Sierpinski gasket, possessing a fractional Hausdorff dimension of $D = \frac{\ln 3}{\ln 2} \approx 1.585$.

Modulo Bitwise Tapestries

Applying modulo arithmetic $(x \oplus y \oplus t) \pmod m == 0$ creates crystalline periodic symmetries. Divisors like $m=3, 5, 7$ generate intricate Moorish Islamic tile stars and kaleidoscopic mandalas.

Walsh-Hadamard Transform

XOR fractal matrices are directly isomorphic to Hadamard matrices $H_{2^k}$. Their rows form mutually orthogonal codes, used globally in CDMA mobile communications, radar, and GPS signals.
