1. The Non-Linear Differential Equations

Edward Lorenz derived these equations by performing a 3-mode Galerkin truncation of the Navier-Stokes and heat transfer equations for a 2D fluid layer heated from below (Rayleigh-Bénard convection):

The Lorenz System (1963)
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = \sigma (y - x)$$ $$\frac{dy}{dt} = x (\rho - z) - y$$ $$\frac{dz}{dt} = x y - \beta z$$

The variables represent physical atmospheric state parameters:

  • $x$ (Convective Intensity): Proportional to the circulating velocity of fluid flow.
  • $y$ (Temperature Difference): Proportional to the horizontal temperature gradient between ascending and descending fluid currents.
  • $z$ (Thermal Inversion): Distortion of the vertical temperature profile from pure linearity.

2. Volume Contraction & Dissipation

The Lorenz system is strictly dissipative, meaning phase space volumes shrink exponentially to zero. We can compute the divergence of the vector field:

Phase Space Volume Contraction Proof
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial \dot{x}}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial \dot{y}}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial \dot{z}}{\partial z} = -\sigma - 1 - \beta$$ $$\text{For canonical parameters } \sigma=10, \beta=8/3: \quad \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = -10 - 1 - 2.667 = -13.667 < 0$$ $$V(t) = V(0) e^{-(\sigma + 1 + \beta) t} \to 0 \quad \text{as } t \to \infty$$

Because volume contracts to zero while trajectories remain perpetually bounded in a finite region, all long-term states are compressed onto an object of zero 3D volume: the Strange Attractor.

3. Sensitive Dependence on Initial Conditions (The Butterfly Effect)

Because trajectories continually stretch along the unstable manifold and fold back due to dissipative contraction, any two initial conditions separated by distance $\delta_0 = 10^{-4}$ separate at an average rate governed by the maximal Lyapunov exponent:

$$\delta(t) \sim \delta_0 e^{\lambda_{\max} t}, \quad \lambda_{\max} \approx 0.9056$$

This exponential separation means that after a finite prediction horizon $t_{\text{horizon}} \sim \frac{1}{\lambda} \ln(1 / \delta_0)$, microscopic quantum or thermal fluctuations render deterministic long-term weather forecasting mathematically impossible.
