When an arrow arcs across the sky or an artillery shell seeks its target, motion escapes the confines of a single straight line and expands into the plane. Discovered by Galileo Galilei, the foundational law governing all multi-dimensional motion is the independence of orthogonal dimensions: what occurs horizontally has zero influence on vertical gravitational free fall. Eliminating time yields the timeless inverted parabola, whose family of curves defines an impenetrable geometric boundary: the parabolic safety envelope.

The Architectural Principles of 2D Ballistics

1. Orthogonal Independence

$$\mathbf{r}(t) = [v_0 \cos\theta \cdot t] \mathbf{\hat{i}} + [v_0 \sin\theta \cdot t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2] \mathbf{\hat{j}}$$. Horizontal motion proceeds at constant speed, completely unaffected by vertical acceleration.

2. The Timeless Parabola

Eliminating $$t = \frac{x}{v_0 \cos\theta}$$ yields the trajectory: $$y(x) = x \tan\theta - \frac{g}{2v_0^2 \cos^2\theta} x^2$$. Its apex occurs where vertical velocity vanishes ($$v_y \equiv 0$$).

3. The Parabolic Safety Envelope

The upper envelope of all possible trajectories for fixed $$v_0$$ is the bounding parabola: $$y_{\text{bound}}(x) = \frac{v_0^2}{2g} - \frac{g}{2v_0^2}x^2$$. Targets outside this envelope are strictly unreachable.

4. The Leap into Orbit

As horizontal launch velocity reaches $$v_{\text{orb}} = \sqrt{g R_E} \approx 7.9\,\text{km/s}$$, the rate of falling matches Earth's curvature. The projectile falls forever without landing: it enters circular orbit.

1. The Geometry of 2D Projectiles

When air resistance is negligible, the equations of motion decouple completely:

$$\begin{aligned} x(t) &= v_0 \cos\theta \cdot t \\ y(t) &= v_0 \sin\theta \cdot t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \end{aligned}$$

Solving the horizontal relation for $t = \frac{x}{v_0 \cos\theta}$ and substituting into the vertical trajectory produces the timeless parabola:

The Parabolic Trajectory Equation
$$y(x) = x \tan\theta - \frac{g}{2v_0^2 \cos^2\theta} x^2$$

2. Key Trajectory Metrics

  • Time of Flight ($T$): When $y = 0$, $T = \frac{2v_0 \sin\theta}{g}$.
  • Maximum Apex Height ($H$): Occurs where $v_y = 0$, giving $H = \frac{v_0^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g}$.
  • Horizontal Range ($R$): $R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}$. The maximum range occurs at $\theta = 45^\circ$, and complementary angles ($\theta$ and $90^\circ - \theta$) achieve identical range.

3. The Parabolic Safety Envelope

By considering all possible launch angles $\theta \in (0, \pi/2)$ for a given launch speed $v_0$, the outer boundary enclosing all reachable space forms an inverted bounding parabola:

$$y_{\text{bound}}(x) = \frac{v_0^2}{2g} - \frac{g}{2v_0^2}x^2$$

Points with coordinates $(x, y)$ satisfying $y > y_{\text{bound}}(x)$ cannot be reached by any launch angle with speed $v_0$.
