For millennia, human philosophy drew an impenetrable wall between the corruptible earth below and the divine, circular heavens above. In 1687, Isaac Newton shattered this division forever with a singular, radical realization: the mundane force pulling a ripe apple to the orchard grass is identical to the invisible tether bending the Moon into its eternal celestial orbit. By unifying earthly dynamics with Johannes Kepler's empirical planetary observations, Newton established the foundation of modern physics: Universal Gravitation.

The Celestial Mechanics Architecture

Newton's Mountain Cannon

$$v_{\text{orb}} = \sqrt{\frac{G M}{R}} \approx 7.91 \text{ km/s}$$. An orbit is not zero gravity: it is perpetual falling. When forward speed matches the curve of the globe, the falling object drops by 4.9 meters for every 8,000 meters traveled forward, continuously missing the ground.

The Inverse-Square Well

$$\mathbf{F} = -G \frac{M m}{r^2} \hat{\mathbf{r}}$$. Gravitational flux spreads across expanding spherical shells ($$A = 4\pi r^2$$). Double your distance from a world, and the geometric dilution reduces the attractive force to exactly one-quarter ($$1/2^2$$).

Kepler's 1st Law (Ellipses)

$$r(\theta) = \frac{a(1 - e^2)}{1 + e \cos \theta}$$. Planets do not travel in perfect circles; they trace ellipses with the Sun located at one focal point and empty space at the other. Distance fluctuates between perihelion ($$a(1-e)$$) and aphelion ($$a(1+e)$$).

Kepler's 2nd Law (Equal Areas)

$$\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{L}{2m} = \text{constant}$$. Because gravity pulls purely radially toward the central mass, it exerts zero perpendicular torque ($$\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F} = 0$$). Angular momentum is invariant, forcing the planet to sweep equal areas in equal times.

The Mathematical Derivation

Consider a satellite of mass $$m$$ moving at distance $$r$$ around a central spherical mass $$M$$. In Newtonian mechanics, the required inward centripetal acceleration to sustain circular motion must be supplied exclusively by the gravitational field:

$$\frac{m v^2}{r} = \frac{G M m}{r^2} \implies v_{\text{orb}} = \sqrt{\frac{G M}{r}}$$

For an elliptical orbit with semi-major axis $$a$$, total mechanical energy $$E = K + U = \frac{1}{2}m v^2 - \frac{GMm}{r} = -\frac{GMm}{2a}$$ is strictly conserved. This yields the renowned Vis-Viva Equation:

$$v^2 = G M \left( \frac{2}{r} - \frac{1}{a} \right)$$

Setting total energy $$E = 0$$ gives the condition where the object carries enough kinetic energy to coast to infinite distance, deriving the universal Escape Velocity:

$$v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 G M}{R}} = \sqrt{2} \, v_{\text{orb}} \approx 11.18 \text{ km/s for Earth}$$

To prove Kepler's Third Law ($$T^2 \propto a^3$$), we integrate the constant areal velocity $$\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{L}{2m}$$ across one complete orbital period $$T$$. The total area of an ellipse is $$A = \pi a b$$, where $$b = a\sqrt{1 - e^2}$$ and specific angular momentum $$h = L/m = \sqrt{G M a (1 - e^2)}$$:

$$T = \frac{2 \pi a b}{h} = \frac{2 \pi a \cdot a\sqrt{1-e^2}}{\sqrt{GM a (1-e^2)}} = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{GM}} a^{3/2} \implies T^2 = \left( \frac{4\pi^2}{G M} \right) a^3$$
