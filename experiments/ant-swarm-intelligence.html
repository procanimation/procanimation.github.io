A single ant possesses virtually zero individual intelligence. It has no map, no long-term memory, and no concept of geometry. Yet, a colony of thousands of ants functions as a distributed, fault-tolerant supercomputer capable of solving the Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP) and routing around obstacles faster than classical algorithms.

This phenomenon is governed by stigmergy—a mechanism of indirect coordination where the traces left in the environment by an action stimulate the performance of subsequent actions.

The Swarm Lexicon

Stigmergy

Indirect communication mediated by environmental modifications (chemical pheromone deposits) rather than direct agent-to-agent signalling.

Evaporation ($\rho$)

The continuous exponential decay of pheromone trails preventing the swarm from freezing into obsolete, suboptimal routes.

Positive Feedback

Shorter paths allow faster transit cycles, accumulating pheromones exponentially faster than longer detour paths.

ACO Transition Rule

Dorigo's probabilistic choice equation: $p_{ij} = \frac{\tau_{ij}^\alpha \eta_{ij}^\beta}{\sum \tau_{ik}^\alpha \eta_{ik}^\beta}$, balancing trail strength $\tau$ with distance heuristic $\eta$.

Mathematical Formulation (Marco Dorigo, 1992)

In classical graph search, the decision of which edge to traverse is formulated probabilistically. Let $\tau_{ij}$ represent the pheromone intensity on the edge between vertex $i$ and $j$, and let $\eta_{ij} = \frac{1}{d_{ij}}$ represent the heuristic distance desirability. The transition probability for agent $k$ is given by:

$$\mathcal{P}_{ij}^k(t) = \frac{\left[\tau_{ij}(t)\right]^\alpha \cdot \left[\eta_{ij}\right]^\beta}{\sum_{l \in \mathcal{N}_i^k} \left[\tau_{il}(t)\right]^\alpha \cdot \left[\eta_{il}\right]^\beta}$$

Trail updates occur through continuous chemical deposit and linear evaporation:

$$\tau_{ij}(t + 1) = (1 - \rho)\tau_{ij}(t) + \sum_{k=1}^{m} \Delta \tau_{ij}^k(t)$$

Where $\Delta \tau_{ij}^k = \frac{Q}{L_k}$ if agent $k$ traverses edge $(i, j)$ of total path length $L_k$. Because $L_{\text{short}} < L_{\text{long}}$, the deposit rate per unit time is strictly superior on shorter geodesics, establishing an inescapable mathematical attractor toward optimality.
