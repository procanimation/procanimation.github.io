1. The Schrödinger Eigenvalue Problem in Parabolic Potentials

The Hamiltonian operator for a one-dimensional particle in a quadratic harmonic potential is:

The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator Hamiltonian
$$\hat{H} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dx^2} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2$$

Introducing the dimensionless coordinate $\xi = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}}x$ and energy eigenvalues $\varepsilon = \frac{2E}{\hbar\omega}$:

$$\frac{d^2\psi}{d\xi^2} + (\varepsilon - \xi^2)\psi = 0$$

The asymptotic solution for large $\xi$ decays as $e^{-\xi^2/2}$. Factoring out this Gaussian envelope, $\psi(\xi) = H(\xi) e^{-\xi^2/2}$, transforms the equation into the classical Hermite differential equation:

$$H'' - 2\xi H' + (\varepsilon - 1)H = 0$$

For the series to terminate into a polynomial of degree $n$, the eigenvalue must satisfy $\varepsilon - 1 = 2n$, enforcing the quantized energy ladder:

$$E_n = \hbar\omega\left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right), \quad n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$$

2. Dirac's Ladder Operator Algebra

In operator mechanics, Dirac defined the non-Hermitian creation and annihilation operators:

$$\hat{a} = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat{x} + \frac{i}{m\omega}\hat{p}\right), \quad \hat{a}^\dagger = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat{x} - \frac{i}{m\omega}\hat{p}\right)$$

Satisfying the canonical commutation relation $[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 1$. The Hamiltonian operator condenses into the number operator $\hat{N} = \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$:

$$\hat{H} = \hbar\omega\left(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \hbar\omega\left(\hat{N} + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$
